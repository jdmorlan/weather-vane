Vane

Your weather station, explained.

A weather app for a Tempest station that says why, not just what — read from your own years of readings. For Mac, iPhone, iPad and Apple TV.

In beta on TestFlight · not on the App Store yet

It teaches, not just displays

Every view is built to answer a question someone actually has, with the numbers printed on the picture — and wherever it can, with a mechanism you can check in your own data.

Rain

This year's running total against the usual one, with each month's share of the gap. How long since it rained, and whether the last thirty days are normal for now. The US Drought Monitor's weekly rating for your county. What's coming, in hours and days.

Below that, every year on record as a row on the calendar: each rain day, every dry spell of two weeks or more, the biggest days labelled.

Forecast

The next three days as one figure: temperature, dew point, rain, wind and storms on a shared clock. A lane appears only when it has something to say, and the ones that stood down are named in a line above it.

Rain for the next eighteen hours is asked of eleven models at once — the blend, the high-resolution short-range model, eight globals and an AI model — with the disagreement left in, because "4 of 11" is something you can argue with and "36%" isn't.

Map

Radar straight from the nearest NEXRAD site, satellite, storm and flood outlooks, rain totals, fronts and burn bans — each layer named and dated by its source, never blended into something that looks like a measurement and isn't.

Rhythms and Explore

When is it hot here? When can it freeze? When does it rain hard? Each is answered from your own station's years as a place on the calendar: when it's the norm, when it's possible at all, and this season so far.

Explore takes any question further — type it in words with ⌘K, and get the dates, the readings and every year side by side.

And

  • Compare and History — any stretch against the same stretch in other years, and one stretch's story.
  • Wind — where it comes from, by season and by hour, from every minute on record.
  • Forecast check — how close the ten-day has been to what the station measured.
  • Your rain gauge — log your own gauge, and see what it says about the station's sensor.
  • Station — is it reporting, is the battery holding, are all six sensors answering.

What you need

A Tempest weather station and a personal access token from your Tempest account — it's read-only. Everything else is free public data.

Where the data comes from

  • Your Tempest station, through WeatherFlow
  • National Weather Service: forecasts, watches and warnings
  • NEXRAD Level II radar, through Unidata on AWS
  • GOES-19 and GOES-18 satellite, through NOAA nowCOAST
  • Storm Prediction Center and Weather Prediction Center outlooks
  • Open-Meteo: model rain and the upper-air pattern
  • Airport reports, through aviationweather.gov
  • US Drought Monitor
  • Texas A&M Forest Service: burn bans
